# The lock-free pipe lane is unused and enabling it gains nothing

Verified (2026-11-18, #4507): the lock-free first lane that the Rust `pipe.rs` once had (single-producer queue, "takes no lock at all") was reachable only from Rust tests, and is deleted; the C pipe (`src/runtime/core/pipe.c`) takes a spin lock per cell. Every stdlib pipe closes the `Pipe`'s own writer and then calls `msrt_pipe_add_writer` from `writer()`, which sets `multi`, so all writes take the locked lane. Letting a first writer keep the lane (add a writer only flips `multi` while another end is open) gave no change: 4M `Int` across threads 670 vs 670 ms, request and reply 1100 vs 1100 ms. The cross-thread cost is about 150 ns per item of cache lines moving between cores, not the lock or the `SeqCst` fences (removing both changed nothing). Go does the same transfer in about 70 ns.

It also fails: `claim_reader` loads `waiting` and swaps it later, so a reader that has already taken the value through `Next::Again` and registered its next read can be claimed for a value that is gone, and `lane_pop` returns none ("a claimed reader finds the value just written" panics, 3 of 3 runs of a 4M-value producer and consumer). Do not bring a lock-free lane back for speed.

Also measured and not worth it: spinning 2 us before a cross-thread read or flush parks (request and reply 1100 to 620 ms, throughput unchanged), and scalar values inline in the queue item (no change). Real worker pools with 0.4 us of work per item run at Go's speed.

Apply when: changing pipe synchronization, or tempted to speed up cross-thread `Pipe` throughput.
Evidence: [`bench/perf/perf-bash-2026-11-18-concurrency.md`](https://github.com/manuscript-lang/manuscript/blob/48b25d38/bench/perf/perf-bash-2026-11-18-concurrency.md), `src/runtime/core/pipe.c`.
Verify: 4M `Int` through one writer and one reader on two threads (`MS_THREADS=2`) against `MS_THREADS=1`.

Update (same day): the cross-thread cost was lock contention, not cache lines alone. The reader copied a batch under the lock and polled it three times per empty read, so the writer spun and called `sched_yield`. Swapping buffers under the lock, an unlocked empty poll, a cache line for the queue length and inline scalar items took the 4M `Int` median from 698 to 546 ms (Go 310). What is left: a reader that keeps catching up parks and wakes per value (request and reply 1177 ms across threads, 320 on one; Go 180 either way because it keeps the pair on one thread), and spinning before the park trades that against throughput.

Second update (same day): the remaining cross-thread cost was the reader polling, not parking or the lock itself. Parks stayed under 2,500 per 4M values; the run time followed the number of reader batches (26k batches 89 ms, 520k batches 654 ms) because a reader that keeps pace looks at the queue length every ~100 ns, and each look moves the line the writer stores to. `msrt_pipe_brief_wait` makes the reader look at doubling gaps (first gap adaptive per pipe, 100 ns to 16 us, 20 us in total) and never wait when it is the only thread or has other runnable tasks. 4M `Int` 575 to 342 ms (Go 310), request and reply 994 to 476 (Go 193). A fixed gap trades one against the other (16 us: 145 ms and 1,518 ms), so keep the feedback; check both numbers and `MS_THREADS=1` after any change.

Third update (same day): the rest of the gap to Go was placement, not synchronization. Go keeps a new goroutine on its creator's thread, so a producer and the consumer waiting on it hand over by a task switch. Here a new promise waited up to 1 ms for a helper thread, so the pair ran on two threads and every hand-over was a wake (request and reply 1.2 us against 0.54 us on one thread). `own_ready` now lets a thread that runs no promise and has no timer, time limit or descriptor waiter start its own promise at once; with the timer condition removed, a sleeping main starts late by the length of the promise (1.57 s measured), because tasks are never preempted. 4M `Int` 332 to 127 ms, request and reply 467 to 210 ms (Go 325 and 198). Check `resp`-style probes (a main sleeping 1 ms while a promise computes) with any change to `own_ready`.
