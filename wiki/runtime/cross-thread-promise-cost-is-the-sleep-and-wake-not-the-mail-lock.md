# Cross-thread promise cost is the sleep and wake, not the mail lock

Verified (2026-11-06, x86-64 Linux sandbox, 4 cores): a promise cost about 3.5 µs and 5 acquisitions of a slot's mail lock, 8% of them contended. A lock-free event inbox (atomic-swap Treiber stack) made no difference. The cost was the wake path: `isolate::kick` took three mutexes, two global (`REGISTRY`, `IDLE`), and wrote an eventfd for 90% of wake-ups whose target was not sleeping. Making the registry and the idle count lock-free and writing the eventfd only when the target is about to block (`isolate::set_sleeping`, re-checked after setting) made it slower (5.4 µs), because threads then slept and woke through `epoll_wait` for every promise; adding a 20 µs spin before sleeping (`task::spin_for_work`, skipped with one scheduler thread) reached 1.9 µs. So the order matters: remove the lock only together with something that keeps a just-idle thread awake.
Apply when: touching `isolate.rs` idle accounting, `sched::wake`, or `reactor_wait`; a poster publishes its work, then unparks, then wakes if the target is flagged sleeping; a sleeper flags, then looks for work once more.
Evidence: [`bench/perf/mailbox/README.md`](../../bench/perf/mailbox/README.md).
Verify: `ms run bench/perf/mailbox/main.ms -- 100000 5`; `cargo test -p ms-rt remote_posts_never_miss_a_sleeping_scheduler`.

Pipes (2026-11-08): a pipe transfer between threads cost 5 to 10 times a same-thread one because every item took the one table lock of all pipes three times and, contended, each hand-over was a futex wait and wake. The table lock is now a spin lock (a few pointer moves under it) and a write answers `FULL` itself instead of a second `msrt_pipe_buffered` call; the same pipeline runs 0.075 s typically (was 0.14 s best, 0.5 to 1.4 s otherwise). Evidence: [`bench/perf/pipe-pipeline/README.md`](../../bench/perf/pipe-pipeline/README.md).

Pipes, what is left (2026-11-08, #2926, 4 vCPUs): the pipeline is bimodal by placement. All tasks on one thread runs 0.055 to 0.07 s. With tasks on different threads the cost is contended acquisitions of the pipe lock: counting them gave 0 contended acquisitions at 0.08 s, 0.2 to 0.7 M at 0.3 to 0.55 s and 2.5 M of 5 M at 1.3 to 1.7 s. A bare spin lock fought over by 2, 3 and 4 threads costs 68, 147 and 166 ns per acquisition on this machine (scratch microbenchmark), so 2 M writes cost 0.14 to 0.33 s before any other work. Tried and measured no wall-clock gain: a lock per pipe in cache-line-aligned cells found by a lock-free table, and a reader that takes up to `capacity` queued values (at most 256) per acquisition into a queue only it touches. Acquisitions fell from 4.1 M to 2.1 M, but interleaved runs against the old binary were equal (medians 0.4 s both; the fast one-thread mode became rarer). Not tried, because each changes what the reader can observe: per-thread single-producer rings merged by the reader (cross-writer order would follow ring order, not real time unless every item takes a sequence stamp from one shared counter), or keeping the tasks of one pipe on one thread. Do not retry the per-pipe lock and reader batch alone.
